# AY 2026-27 Minor Child Income Clubbing Checklist

A minor's bank or investment account can contain gifts, interest, dividends, gains, or earned income with different clubbing consequences. Reporting only the closing balance or bank interest can miss the underlying source and parent-level treatment.

List each income-producing asset, identify who transferred or funded it, and compare the child statement with the relevant parent's return records before applying clubbing or any exemption.

## Trace each child's income back to the asset and funding source

List every income-producing account or asset held for the minor: deposits, mutual funds, shares, property interests, or work-related receipts. For each one, record who funded or transferred the asset, who controls it, the income earned, and where the money was credited. The closing balance and the child's bank-interest total do not reveal whether the income arose from a family gift, inherited asset, reinvestment, or the child's own activity.

Use that source map to decide whether an amount belongs in a parent's computation or needs separate treatment. Keep income from the child's own skill, work, or performance visible rather than grouping it with passive investment income. If both parents may be relevant, compare the family and prior-return facts before selecting the reporting position, and document any change from earlier years. Later reinvestment should remain linked to its original source so accumulated income is not mistaken for new independent funding. Pause for changing custody, disputed beneficial ownership, foreign accounts, or pooled family investments where the child's units and income cannot be reliably identified.

## Read child bank statement, investment statement, and parent Form 16 for different facts

- **Child bank statement:** Child bank statement proves the date and net movement of money relevant to minor child income clubbing; it rarely proves the whole tax treatment. Connect each material credit or debit to investment statement and explain transfers, withholding, or non-income amounts.
- **Investment statement:** Investment statement is a source ledger for the return working, but its labels and totals still need interpretation. Tie the relevant rows to parent Form 16, preserve the original export, and document exclusions or adjustments separately.
- **Parent Form 16:** Read parent Form 16 for the salary, perquisite, deduction, and TDS facts it actually contains for the return working. Test those figures against correction trail and filing acknowledgement and add income or corrections the employer record does not cover.

## Resolve child bank statement and investment statement differences before filing

Escalate disputed source of funds, income from the child's own skill or work, changing custody or parent facts, foreign assets, or records that do not identify the beneficial owner.

Before submitting, check clubbing, bank interest, investments, exemptions, and disclosure trail. Record what parent Form 16 establishes, explain any remaining difference, and retain the correction trail and filing acknowledgement with the final computation.

## Record the child's income source before assigning it to a parent

Prepare a child-level schedule showing each bank account, deposit, investment, asset, gift, award, activity, and resulting income. For every item, record who funded or transferred the asset, who controls it, what income arose, and whether an exception may need review. Do not combine the child's own skill- or talent-based earnings with passive income merely because both reached the same account.

Where clubbing applies, document why the selected parent's return carries the amount and how any relevant exemption or separate reporting is handled. Compare the child and parent records with AIS and tax-credit statements without creating duplicate income. Preserve gift, investment, bank, activity, custody, and calculation records so the treatment can be revisited when ownership, income type, or family circumstances change.

Keep the child's own return question separate from the parent's clubbing calculation. Tax credit, account ownership, or income earned through the child's skill may require a different treatment from passive income arising on a transferred asset.
