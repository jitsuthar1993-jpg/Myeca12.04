# GST Registration, Query Reply, Certificate, and First Compliance Steps

Getting the GSTIN is step one. What most businesses underestimate is everything that follows — the invoicing format, the return calendar, the ITC reconciliation process, and the record-keeping discipline that determines whether the registration actually serves the business or just creates paperwork.

The practical mistake is to treat registration approval as the finish line. It is the starting line.

## Does your business actually need to register?

Before filing an application, work out whether registration is mandatory or whether voluntary registration makes business sense. The triggers for mandatory registration include crossing the applicable turnover threshold, making interstate supplies in certain situations, selling through ecommerce platforms, being liable under reverse charge, and other conditions specified in GST law.

State, activity type, and supply model all affect the answer. A business that registers without understanding its position often ends up with compliance obligations it is not prepared for. A business that delays past the mandatory threshold creates a different set of problems. Sort this out before applying.

## Organizing your documents before you apply

A clean application that goes through without queries saves days of back-and-forth.

| File area | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Applicant KYC | PAN, Aadhaar, photo, contact details |
| Entity proof | Incorporation certificate, partnership deed, LLP agreement, authorization |
| Premises proof | Rent agreement, owner NOC, electricity bill, ownership proof |
| Bank proof | Cancelled cheque, bank statement, account details |
| Activity details | Goods or services, HSN or SAC context, ecommerce or export notes |

If the business does not yet have clear sales records, a confirmed premises address, or a functional bank account, those gaps tend to surface as officer queries. Better to resolve them before applying.

## When the officer raises a query

A query is not a rejection — it is a request for clarification. Respond to the specific point raised, not with a random bundle of documents. If the query is about premises, provide address proof. If it is about the business activity, provide an explanation note with supporting documents. Match each query precisely to the evidence that answers it.

Keep the acknowledgement of the query response. If the application is approved later, that trail shows how the issue was resolved.

## GST registration: What to do once the GSTIN arrives

Receiving the certificate means the compliance clock has started. Before the first return period ends, the business should have:

- An invoicing format that includes GSTIN, correct tax rates, HSN or SAC codes, and place of supply
- A return filing calendar (GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B at minimum)
- A process for collecting and recording purchase invoices
- A clear understanding of which ITC is eligible and which is blocked
- Secure custody of the GST portal login

GSTR-2B reconciliation should become part of the monthly routine from the very first period. Waiting to build this habit creates gaps that are difficult to close retroactively.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is GST registration required for every business?

No. Applicability depends on activity, turnover, supply model, state, ecommerce route, and specific GST rules. Voluntary registration may also be considered.

### What happens if the GST officer raises a query?

The applicant should respond with clear supporting records such as premises proof, identity records, business explanation, bank proof, or authorization documents.

### For GST registration, what should be done after GSTIN is issued?

Set up invoicing, return calendar, HSN or SAC review, login custody, and record preservation before the first compliance period.
