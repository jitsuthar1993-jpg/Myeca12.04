# AY 2026-27 Foreign Asset Disclosure Checklist for Residents

Foreign accounts, shares, employer awards, property, income, and foreign tax can require different schedules and reporting periods. A year-end portfolio value does not answer every Schedule FA, FSI, TR, or capital-gain question.

Create one inventory row per foreign account or asset, then connect ownership dates, statement values, income, withholding, and any sale to the relevant return schedule and calculation.

## Map each foreign holding to its disclosure and income questions

Create one inventory row per foreign bank account, broker account, employer share plan, company interest, property, pension, or other asset. Record country, institution, ownership basis, opening and closing dates, statements available, income, withholding, sale activity, and the values needed for review. A year-end screenshot cannot establish ownership throughout the period, peak or other requested values, income earned, or acquisition cost.

For each row, separately answer whether it creates an asset disclosure, foreign-income entry, tax-relief question, or capital-gain calculation. Link those workings without combining them; reporting an account does not report its income, and reporting a sale does not settle the remaining holding. Explicitly review dormant, joint, beneficially owned, inherited, and employer-linked accounts. Preserve the original statements and the exchange-rate method used so values can be reproduced. Pause where residential status is uncertain, historical reports are unavailable, an old employer account remains accessible but unexplained, or ownership cannot be distinguished from mere signing authority. Do not omit the row or invent a value simply to complete a schedule field.

## Read foreign account statement, broker report, and Form 67 records for different facts

- **Foreign account statement:** Foreign account statement is a source ledger for foreign asset disclosure, but its labels and totals still need interpretation. Tie the relevant rows to broker report, preserve the original export, and document exclusions or adjustments separately.
- **Broker report:** Broker report is a source ledger for the open tax issue, but its labels and totals still need interpretation. Tie the relevant rows to Form 67 records, preserve the original export, and document exclusions or adjustments separately.
- **Form 67 records:** Use Form 67 records for the person, period, amount, or filing fact it directly establishes for the evidence review. Compare that fact with correction trail and filing acknowledgement, and keep any unresolved difference visible in the working before deciding how to check Schedule FA, foreign income, tax credit, and records for overseas accounts or shares.

## Resolve foreign account statement and broker report differences before filing

Pause for uncertain residential status, missing historical statements, joint or beneficial ownership, employer plans, unavailable exchange-rate evidence, or assets that cannot be mapped to a schedule field.

Before submitting, check Schedule FA, foreign income, tax credit, and records for overseas accounts or shares. Record what Form 67 records establish, explain any remaining difference, and retain the correction trail and filing acknowledgement with the final computation.

## Inventory foreign accounts and assets before opening the schedules

Create one row per foreign bank account, broker account, share plan, company interest, property, pension, insurance product, or other relevant asset. Record the country, institution, ownership basis, opening and closing dates, statement period, values required for review, income, withholding, disposal, and source of the information. A year-end portfolio screenshot cannot answer ownership dates, peak or other required values, income, or sale calculations.

Connect each asset to the relevant income, gain, foreign-tax, and disclosure working without merging those questions. Review joint, beneficial, inherited, dormant, and employer-linked holdings explicitly. Preserve original statements and the exchange-rate method used for each calculation. Where historical records are missing, keep the gap visible and seek case-specific analysis before filing; do not omit an asset or invent a value merely to complete the schedule.
