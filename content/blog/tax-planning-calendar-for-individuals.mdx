# A Month-by-Month Tax Planning Calendar for Indian Individuals

Good tax planning is rarely about a single decision taken in March. It is about a set of smaller actions spread across the year that together reduce unnecessary tax, avoid interest and penalties, and make filing week feel less like a crisis. For most salaried individuals, the year-end scramble happens because the groundwork was never laid. This calendar is designed to change that pattern.

## April to June: Open the year properly

The first quarter is the most useful time to influence the full year. Begin by creating a fresh folder for FY 2026-27 — separate from the folder you are just closing. Review your salary structure with your HR or payroll system to understand how much of your income is variable, how much is structured as allowances, and whether your current regime choice still makes sense given any salary revisions.

Estimate your likely annual income: salary, any freelance or interest income, expected capital gains from mutual funds or shares. If the total points to a tax liability after TDS, check whether quarterly advance tax payments will be required. Setting a calendar reminder for 15 June, 15 September, 15 December, and 15 March costs nothing and avoids interest under Section 234B and 234C.

This is also the time to make the old-vs-new regime decision — not in February. The right choice depends on your actual deductions, not a generic calculator.

## July to September: File, then learn from what you filed

July is the month most individual taxpayers are focused on filing the previous year's return. File before the due date, save the acknowledgement, and keep the computation. But before moving on, take ten minutes to review what the previous year's return actually showed. Did you pay significant advance tax? Was there a TDS mismatch? Did you miss a deduction?

The filing result is a feedback loop. If you claimed 80C deductions of only Rs 50,000 when the limit is Rs 1,50,000, ask why — and whether it would make sense to increase contributions this year. If you had bank interest that was not fully captured in the prefill, set a reminder to download Form 26AS in October rather than waiting.

## October to December: Reconcile the mid-year picture

By October, roughly half the financial year has passed. Download AIS and Form 26AS and review them against your own records. Check TDS from salary, bank interest, client payments, and investments. If any deductor has not reported correctly, now is the time to raise it — waiting until March leaves no room to chase a correction.

Review capital gains from any assets sold during the year. Mutual fund, share, and property transactions should be classified by holding period and asset type before year-end. Applying losses against gains is easier when the numbers are current rather than reconstructed in March.

## January to March: Close the year properly

Finish any planned investments under 80C, 80D, or NPS before 31 March. Collect supporting documents now: rent receipts from the landlord, health insurance receipts, home loan certificates, donation receipts for 80G. Do not wait for employers to ask — have the proofs ready to submit.

If advance tax is required for the fourth quarter, pay by 15 March. Check the advance tax calculation against current-year income, especially if there have been capital gains or variable income not covered by TDS.

## Core planning areas at a glance

| Area | What to review |
| --- | --- |
| Regime choice | Old vs new based on deductions and salary structure |
| 80C | EPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance, tuition fees, principal repayment |
| 80D | Health insurance and preventive health checkup |
| NPS | Additional deduction and employer contribution |
| HRA | Rent, landlord details, city, salary structure |
| Capital gains | Holding period, loss set-off, statements |
| Interest income | Savings, FD, bonds, and TDS |

## The document file that makes all of this manageable

Tax planning without documents is just guesswork. For a salaried individual, the core file should include Form 16, Form 16A where relevant, AIS and TIS, Form 26AS, bank interest certificates, rent proofs, health insurance receipts, home loan certificates, investment proofs, capital gains reports, donation receipts, challans, and the final ITR acknowledgement. Business owners should add invoices, GST returns, bank statements, and expense records.

The one habit that matters most is a one-page computation note: total income, deductions claimed, taxes already paid, balance payable or refund expected, and the return form used. This note is what makes a confusing AIS entry or a lender's request easy to handle six months later.

## When something goes wrong

Tax and compliance situations do not follow a single script. Before filing, the job is to collect records, reconcile AIS and Form 26AS, choose the correct form, and review what is payable or refundable. After filing but before processing, complete e-verification and preserve the acknowledgement. If a mistake surfaces after filing or a notice arrives, check which remedy fits:

| Situation | Recommended route |
| --- | --- |
| Before filing ITR | Collect records, reconcile AIS/Form 26AS, choose the correct form, and review tax payable or refund. |
| After filing but before processing | Complete e-verification and preserve acknowledgement and computation. |
| Mistake or notice found | Check revised return, rectification, grievance, updated return, or notice response based on the document. |

These routes are not interchangeable. Using rectification when a revised return is the right answer, or filing an updated return without checking eligibility, creates additional problems. The route depends on the document, the timing, and what the department has already done.

## Common planning mistakes

| Mistake | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Acting from memory | Approximate salary, investment, or bank numbers do not support a defensible filing position. |
| Using only one record | Form 16 may miss bank interest; AIS may contain duplicates; broker reports may need classification. |
| Choosing the wrong remedy | Revised return, rectification, updated return, grievance, and notice response solve different problems. |
| Weak documentation | Missing rent proof, investment receipts, broker statements, challans, or portal downloads makes later review harder. |

## When to ask for help

Most salaried returns with straightforward income, standard deductions, and clean TDS matching can be filed without professional help. But if there is a large refund claim, AIS mismatch, capital gains from multiple instruments, foreign income, rental income, or a notice pending from a prior year, a CA review is genuinely useful — not because the rules are complicated, but because the interaction between records, AIS entries, and filing positions is where errors typically hide.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I choose between old and new tax regime?

Review it early in the year and again before employer declaration or filing. The best choice depends on salary structure and actual deductions.

### Do salaried people need advance tax?

Sometimes. If income outside salary creates tax liability not covered by TDS, advance tax may apply.

## CA technical notes

For tax planning articles, the CA review should verify the old versus new regime comparison, eligible deductions, HRA support, 80C/80D/NPS evidence, advance tax exposure, capital gains, interest income, AIS entries, and whether planning decisions are supported before March-end.

The technical file should end with a concise review note covering the documents checked, the computation method, the filing or compliance route selected, unresolved assumptions, and the next deadline. If the guide is applied to an actual taxpayer, the CA should preserve the calculation sheet, portal downloads, proof index, acknowledgement, and any communication trail. If the case involves a notice, GST mismatch, capital gains, foreign asset, large refund, or return correction, the note should also record the limitation period and the reason the chosen route is better than alternatives.
