# TDS Return Filing Checklist for Employers and Vendors

TDS compliance is a quarterly discipline. Employers and businesses making vendor payments should not wait until filing day to find PAN details, challans, deduction sections, or payment records. A clean TDS return starts from payroll and vendor payment controls.

The goal is to make the deductor's return match the tax deposited and the deductee's expected credit.

## Begin with the filing scope

Before preparing any data, confirm the basics: the quarter, TAN, deductor name, form type (Form 24Q for salary, Form 26Q for non-salary), and whether any prior-quarter correction return is still open. Keep employee data and vendor or professional-fee data in separate working files — combining them too early causes section-mapping errors that take time to unravel.

Map each payment category to a deduction section. If a particular payment type is genuinely ambiguous, resolve it against the applicable provision before it goes into the return file rather than forcing it into a catch-all.

## Prepare deductee and challan data

| File area | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Deductee records | PAN, name, employee or vendor code, contact details |
| Payment records | Invoice, salary register, payment date, amount |
| Deduction details | Section, rate, deduction date, tax amount |
| Challans | BSR code, challan serial number, payment date, amount |
| Filing archive | FVU or return file, acknowledgement, correction notes |

Work through each column systematically. Missing or incorrect PAN is the most common source of credit mismatch: the deductee's Form 26AS will not reflect the tax deducted, and they will follow up. Fixing PAN errors through correction returns after filing is possible but time-consuming.

## Validate before submission

Run through these before generating the FVU file: PAN format and validity, section mapping against the payment type, challan consumption (every rupee deducted should map to a challan), short deduction or short payment calculations, applicable interest under Section 201 or 206C, late filing fee under Section 234E if the due date has passed, and whether any earlier correction return has been accepted on the portal.

If a challan is for the wrong period or has excess balance, that needs to be addressed directly — do not file over a challan discrepancy.

## After filing

Download and preserve the acknowledgement, the filed return file, copies of all challans, deductee-level workings, and any correction notes. When an employee or vendor queries why their Form 26AS credit is short or missing, a well-maintained archive reduces the response time from days to minutes.

## Where TDS filing needs a document-based review

Use a documented workflow to organise TDS data, validate challans, prepare return files, track correction filings, and establish a quarterly compliance rhythm for employers and vendor-paying businesses. For teams handling large payroll runs or multiple vendor categories, structured support reduces errors before they reach the return.

## Frequently asked questions

### What records are needed before TDS return?

Keep deductee PAN, payment details, deduction section, deduction date, challan details, salary or vendor records, and previous acknowledgement records ready.

### Why do PAN errors matter in TDS returns?

Incorrect PAN or deductee details can create credit mismatch for the deductee and correction work for the deductor.

### Should challans be matched before filing?

Yes. Match tax deducted, tax deposited, challan serial number, BSR code, date, and amount before return preparation.

## Close the quarter through a deductee-level reconciliation

Prepare a deductee register showing payment or credit date, nature of payment, section used, threshold or rate basis, gross amount, tax deducted, deduction date, challan allocation, PAN status, and certificate outcome. Reconcile that register with payroll or vendor ledgers and the bank payment trail before uploading the return. A challan total can match while individual deductees, sections, or periods remain wrong.

After filing, compare the acknowledgement and processing status with the register. Track defaults, short deduction, interest, late fee, unmatched challans, and PAN errors as separate correction items. When a correction is filed, preserve the original statement, reason for change, revised data, and new acknowledgement so the tax-credit effect can be explained to the deductee. Close the quarter only after certificates and Form 26AS expectations are reviewed, and escalate disputed classification, missing PAN, cross-quarter adjustments, or payments with unclear withholding treatment before repeating the error.
