# Advance Tax for Tax Year 2026-27 Under the New Act: Checklist

Advance tax for Tax Year 2026-27 is one of those obligations that feels simple until the numbers move. This checklist walks through the estimates, due dates, challans, business income, capital gains, and the paperwork worth keeping along the way.

It is aimed at Indian taxpayers, founders, finance teams, and anyone preparing returns while settling into the Income-tax Act, 2025 and the Finance Act 2025 changes. Treat it as a readiness note rather than a promise of any particular outcome, refund timeline, or freedom from notices. Whatever position you take should still be reconciled against the official portal, the notified forms, and your own records.

## What advance tax really asks of you

Advance tax is paid on what you expect to earn, not on what you earned last year. So the estimate has to be built on current-year figures and revised the moment salary, business profit, rent, interest, capital gains, or TDS assumptions shift.

| Point | Practical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Advance tax should be estimated for Tax Year 2026-27 income. |
| 2 | Salary, business income, interest, rent, and capital gains need separate assumptions. |
| 3 | Challan proof and computation notes should be preserved after each installment. |

## Why the timing matters this year

The transition has created a genuine year-selection headache. In a single month you might be wrapping up Filing the FY 2025-26 return in AY 2026-27, staying current on Tax Year 2026-27 compliance, dealing with an older notice, and learning the new forms all at once. A well-kept file removes that confusion: it shows the period, the law reference, the portal form, whether it is a payment or a return, and the evidence behind each entry.

## A quick example

Take a consultant whose receipts in Tax Year 2026-27 swing from quarter to quarter. Copying last year's return into the advance-tax calculation would be a mistake. The estimate needs to be refreshed each quarter so the installments track what is actually coming in.

## Records worth keeping

- Quarterly income estimate
- TDS/TCS expected credit
- Advance tax challans
- Computation worksheet

## Recalculate advance tax before each instalment

- Forecast taxable income by income head and subtract the TDS and TCS credits reasonably expected for the year.
- Refresh the estimate before each instalment date, recording one-off gains, uneven business receipts, and changed assumptions.
- Pay against the correct PAN and period, then verify that the challan details appear correctly in the tax-credit records.
- Reconcile every estimate and challan with the final return computation before claiming the payments.

## Mistakes that trip people up

- Reaching for a familiar old form number without confirming the current official form.
- Letting AY 2026-27 filing records get mixed in with Tax Year 2026-27 payment or TDS records.
- In the advance tax review, do not treat a headline slab, rebate, or threshold as the completed computation.
- Paying or filing before AIS, Form 26AS, challans, books, and certificates have been reconciled.
- Forgetting to save the official source and the computation note behind the decision.

## Where advance tax Act needs a document-based review

Before choosing an advance-tax instalment, reconcile year-to-date income, tax already deducted, eligible reliefs, and the remaining instalment dates. Use a written estimate that shows the assumptions behind the payment, then revisit it when income or TDS changes materially.

## Advance tax: Before closing the advance tax Act file

Confirm the year, read the official source, gather your supporting records, write a short computation note, decide on the return or payment route, and hold on to the acknowledgement proof once you have submitted.
