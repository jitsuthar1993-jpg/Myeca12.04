# TDS March-April 2026 Transition for Payroll and Vendors

A transition checklist for salary, vendor, rent, professional-fee, and contractor TDS around March 2026 and April 2026 under old and new law references.

For payroll managers and finance teams handling vendor payments, the March-April 2026 window is genuinely tricky. The Income-tax Act, 2025 and Finance Act 2025 have shifted section references and compliance timelines, and both old and new law touchpoints can land on the same desk in the same week. This note is intended to help you sort that out — it is not a substitute for a CA-reviewed position on complex transactions.

## What actually changes at this boundary

The risk in this transition is not just renumbered sections. It runs through every step of the TDS chain: which month a payment falls in, when deduction happens, when the challan is deposited, which return period captures it, and which certificate the deductee eventually receives.

| Point | Practical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | March 2026 and April 2026 payments should be tagged by date and period. |
| 2 | Payroll, vendor, and rent TDS should not be mixed in one transition bucket. |
| 3 | Challans, returns, certificates, and books should reconcile monthly. |

## Why this period is particularly sensitive

AY 2026-27 return work, Tax Year 2026-27 current-period compliance, unresolved notices from earlier years, and newly notified forms can all arrive in the same month. That concentration makes year-selection errors easy to make. A clean file should clearly show the period, the applicable law reference, the portal form used, the nature of the payment or return, and the supporting evidence behind the position taken.

## A practical scenario

Consider a March invoice that gets paid in April. The deductor needs to check whether the deduction timing follows the March billing date or the April payment date, then ensure the challan, accounting entry, and TDS return all tell the same story. Mixing up the period here creates credit mismatches for the vendor and correction work for the deductor.

## Records to maintain

- March and April payment ledgers, separated by category
- Payroll registers showing salary period and deduction date
- Vendor invoices with payment date and deduction details
- TDS challans with BSR code, serial number, and deposit date
- TDS return workings and filed acknowledgements

## Step-by-step checklist

- Identify whether March-April 2026 TDS transition affects AY 2026-27 filing, Tax Year 2026-27 compliance, or both.
- Read the official source and map the rule to your income head, taxpayer type, and dates.
- Collect source records, computation notes, challans, statements, and declarations before filing or payment.
- Check whether the position changes the ITR form, schedule, tax payment, TDS/TCS, or disclosure route.
- Preserve the final return, acknowledgement, e-verification proof, and supporting working papers.

## Errors that tend to show up in this period

- Pulling up an old form number without verifying it is the currently notified form for the applicable period.
- Posting March TDS data into April returns, or vice versa, without checking the deduction date rule.
- Treating a section headline — rate, threshold, or slab — as a complete computation rather than a starting point.
- Filing or depositing before reconciling AIS, Form 26AS, challans, books, and certificates.
- Not retaining the official source and computation note that supported the decision taken.

Confirm the applicable period and law reference. Collect supporting documents. Prepare a short computation note. Verify the payment or return route. And once filed or deposited, preserve the acknowledgement and working papers in a single accessible folder.
