# AY 2026-27 ITR Guide for Lawyers and Legal Consultants

Client payments may arrive after TDS, retainers, reimbursements, court-fee advances, or other deductions. Net bank credits do not show gross professional receipts or decide which costs belong in the practice working.

Compare the client ledger and invoice with Form 16A and the bank receipt. Record the gross fee, TDS, reimbursable amount, and net collection separately before reviewing expenses and form selection.

## Separate earned legal fees from money handled for a matter

Review engagement letters and client ledgers before using bank receipts as professional income. Split retainers, appearance or advisory fees, court-fee advances, filing charges, reimbursable travel, client money held temporarily, and amounts belonging to chambers or a firm. For each fee, bridge the invoice or ledger amount to Form 16A and the net client payment after TDS. This makes it possible to identify gross professional receipts without treating every client-related transfer as earned income.

Use the resulting practice map to decide the return route, expense treatment, and any presumptive question. A lawyer working through a firm, independently, and for foreign clients may need separate streams rather than one occupation label. Test expenses against the actual practice responsibility; a cost paid from the account is not automatically the lawyer's own expense when it was recoverable from a client or borne for another advocate. Pause where trust-like client balances are mixed with fees, chamber allocations are undocumented, foreign receipts lack contracts, or the client and lawyer classify the same payment differently.

## Read Form 16A, client ledger, and bank statement for different facts

- **Form 16A:** Form 16A shows the deductor's gross-payment and TDS reporting for lawyer consultant ITR. Compare its period and amount with client ledger, then keep the gross receipt and tax credit as separate figures in the working.
- **Client ledger:** Client ledger should provide the transaction-level trail for the open tax issue. Test dates, counterparties, narration, and running totals against bank statement, and leave unexplained items visible instead of inserting a balancing adjustment.
- **Bank statement:** Bank statement proves the date and net movement of money relevant to the open tax issue; it rarely proves the whole tax treatment. Connect each material credit or debit to correction trail and filing acknowledgement and explain transfers, withholding, or non-income amounts.

## Resolve Form 16A and client ledger differences before filing

Escalate client money held on behalf of others, mixed chambers or firm income, foreign clients, unsupported expenses, missing TDS, or uncertain presumptive eligibility.

Before submitting, match professional fees, TDS, expenses, and form selection before claiming refunds. Record what bank statement establishes, explain any remaining difference, and retain the correction trail and filing acknowledgement with the final computation.

## Separate client money from professional fees

Build a matter-level receipt register showing the client, engagement, professional fee, retainer, reimbursement, court fee or other client money, TDS, invoice, and net amount received. Amounts held or spent on a client's behalf should not be treated as fees without reviewing the arrangement and ledger, while a net bank credit after TDS does not establish the gross professional receipt.

Reconcile chambers, firm, panel, appearance, advisory, arbitration, and other engagements separately. Review expenses from the actual practice and preserve confidentiality by retaining only the billing and tax evidence needed for filing. Use the full activity to test presumptive eligibility, books, audit questions, GST, credits, and return form. Keep unresolved client-ledger or deductor differences visible rather than balancing them through an unsupported income adjustment.

Where counsel fees pass through a chamber, firm, or instructing advocate, retain the engagement and settlement record that explains the split. Do not expose case papers or client facts that are unnecessary for the tax working.
