# PPF Account Tax Record Checklist for AY 2026-27

Public Provident Fund (PPF) is one of the few investment products in India that carries an Exempt-Exempt-Exempt (EEE) tax structure — contributions qualify for deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act 1961, the interest earned is tax-free, and the maturity proceeds are also exempt. For AY 2026-27 (FY 2025-26), keeping accurate PPF records is particularly relevant if you are claiming the Section 80C deduction under the old tax regime or if your account is approaching its 15-year maturity, a partial withdrawal point, or a loan against the balance.

This checklist covers the documents you need, how to cross-check them against your ITR and AIS, and what to do if there is a mismatch.

## Why PPF record-keeping matters more now

The Finance Act 2025 did not alter the fundamental EEE treatment of PPF, but the shift in default tax regime to the new regime under Section 115BAC means a larger number of taxpayers are no longer automatically opting for the old regime. If you want to claim PPF contributions under Section 80C, you must explicitly choose the old tax regime in your AY 2026-27 ITR. Choosing the wrong regime — or filing without confirming your regime choice — means the deduction goes unclaimed even though you contributed.

Also worth noting: if your PPF account is held at a bank rather than a post office, the bank's statement is your primary record, and it should be cross-checked against the AIS downloaded from the Income Tax portal before filing.

## Quick checklist

- Download your PPF passbook or account statement for FY 2025-26 and confirm total deposits made during the year.
- Verify the contribution amount matches what appears in AIS under the relevant information category.
- Confirm you are filing under the old tax regime if you intend to claim Section 80C deduction for PPF contributions.
- If a partial withdrawal was made during FY 2025-26, note the withdrawal amount and ensure it is reflected correctly — PPF partial withdrawals are tax-free but should not be confused with taxable income.
- If the account completes 15 years in FY 2025-26, confirm the maturity amount and whether it was extended or closed.
- Keep all deposit receipts and bank transfer confirmations — especially for lump-sum contributions made in March 2026 to maximise FY 2025-26 deductions.

## Documents to keep ready

| Document | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| PPF passbook or account statement | Primary proof of contribution amount and dates for FY 2025-26; needed to claim Section 80C deduction. |
| Deposit receipts or bank transfer records | Supports the contribution amount if the passbook entry is delayed or unclear. |
| PAN | Required for linking the PPF account to your ITR and for tax-office queries. |
| Bank account statement | Cross-check the debit entries against PPF deposit receipts to confirm amounts and dates. |
| PAN and bank details | Useful for tax filing, refunds, benefit credits, and identity matching where applicable. |
| A short review note | Records what you checked, what is still pending, and which official source you used. |

## Practical example

A salaried taxpayer contributed ₹1,50,000 to her PPF account in FY 2025-26 — the maximum eligible for Section 80C deduction. She also has LIC premiums of ₹30,000. Since Section 80C has an aggregate ceiling of ₹1,50,000, only the PPF contribution fills the limit, and the LIC premium sits outside. When she downloads her AIS for AY 2026-27, she checks whether the ₹1,50,000 PPF deposit is listed. If it is not, she contacts her bank to confirm the account linkage and PAN seeding before filing.

She also confirms she is choosing the old regime for AY 2026-27, because her new-employer's Form 16 defaulted to the new regime — an easy clerical oversight that, if uncorrected, would mean she loses the ₹1,50,000 deduction entirely.

## Reviewer guidance for PPF cases

When reviewing a PPF file for AY 2026-27, confirm: total contributions during FY 2025-26, whether the contribution figure in AIS matches the passbook, the tax regime selected in the draft ITR, the Section 80C aggregate across all instruments (PPF, LIC, ELSS, NSC, home loan principal), and whether any partial withdrawal or maturity amount needs to be reported or noted.

If the PPF account is jointly affecting multiple financial products — say, a loan against PPF balance that was used for a business purpose — separately note the income head, ITR form, and business income reporting requirements.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Public Provident Fund eligibility guaranteed by this guide?

No. Eligibility depends on the official portal, current scheme rules, state or ministry verification, and the applicant's documents.

### Should I use only social media information before applying?

No. Use social posts only to identify the issue, then verify the rule and application status on official government sources.

### Why keep tax records for a government scheme?

Many applications ask for income, bank, identity, or business records. A clean document trail reduces avoidable mismatch and follow-up questions.

## Final takeaway

PPF's EEE status makes it one of the most tax-efficient savings instruments available to Indian individuals. But claiming that benefit requires choosing the old regime, matching the contribution amount across passbook and AIS, and ensuring your ITR reflects the Section 80C figure correctly. These are not difficult steps — they just need to happen before you file, not after a notice arrives.
