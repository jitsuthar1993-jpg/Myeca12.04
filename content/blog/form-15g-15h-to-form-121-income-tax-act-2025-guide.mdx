# Form 15G/15H to Form 121: New Act Declaration Guide

Understand the form-transition context for no-TDS declarations, how to preserve declarations, and what taxpayers should check under the new law.

For Indian taxpayers adjusting to the Income-tax Act, 2025 and Finance Act 2025 changes, this guide is an educational readiness note — not a promise of tax outcome, refund speed, or notice avoidance. Always match your position against the official portal, notified forms, and your own documents before acting.

## What changed with the new law

Declaration forms prevent or reduce TDS deduction, but only when eligibility is correctly assessed first. Submitting the wrong form — or simply signing the familiar old one without checking the current rules — can create tax, interest, or mismatch problems down the line.

| Point | Practical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | No-TDS declarations need eligibility review, not just form signing. |
| 2 | Banks and deductors should use the correct form for the relevant period. |
| 3 | Taxpayers should preserve acknowledgements and interest certificates. |

## The transition problem in practice

The shift to the Income-tax Act, 2025 creates a year-selection puzzle. AY 2026-27 return work, Tax Year 2026-27 current compliance, old notices, and new forms can all land on your desk in the same month. A clean file should clearly show the period, law reference, portal form, payment or return type, and supporting evidence. Without that clarity, even a straightforward declaration can end up mapped to the wrong year or the wrong legal framework.

## A real-world scenario

A senior citizen giving a no-deduction declaration to a bank still needs to estimate total taxable income for the year. That matters because AIS and Form 26AS will later reflect the interest credited — and if the declared income turns out to be wrong, the mismatch shows up during return processing. Preserving interest statements and the bank's acknowledgement of the declaration is not optional; it is the paper trail that explains your position.

## Records every declarant should keep

- Interest certificate from the bank or deductor
- Copy of the declaration submitted
- Bank acknowledgement of receipt
- AIS and Form 26AS downloads for the relevant period

## Filing checklist

- Identify whether no-TDS declaration filing affects AY 2026-27 filing, Tax Year 2026-27 compliance, or both.
- Read the official source and map the rule to your income head, taxpayer type, and dates.
- Collect source records, computation notes, challans, statements, and declarations before filing or payment.
- Check whether the position changes the ITR form, schedule, tax payment, TDS/TCS, or disclosure route.
- Preserve the final return, acknowledgement, e-verification proof, and supporting working papers.

## Mistakes worth avoiding

The most common error is reaching for a familiar form number out of habit. In a transition year, the correct form for a given period may have changed. Other traps:

- Mixing AY 2026-27 filing records with Tax Year 2026-27 payment or TDS records.
- Treating a headline slab, rebate, or threshold as the final computation rather than a starting point.
- Filing or paying before reconciling AIS, Form 26AS, challans, books, and certificates.
- Not keeping a note of which official source informed the decision.

## In summary

Confirm the year, check the official source, collect supporting records, prepare a short computation note, verify the return or payment route, and preserve the acknowledgement after submission. The declaration is only part of the story; the paper trail behind it is what protects you later.
