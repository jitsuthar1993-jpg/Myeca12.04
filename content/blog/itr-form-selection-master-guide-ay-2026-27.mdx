# ITR Form Selection Master Guide for AY 2026-27

Choose between ITR-1, ITR-2, ITR-3, and ITR-4 for AY 2026-27 with examples for salary, capital gains, business income, house property, and foreign assets.

Most ITR filing errors do not originate at the submission screen. They start much earlier — when the taxpayer locks in the wrong assessment year, relies on a portal prefill without cross-checking, or selects ITR-1 out of habit when the facts point elsewhere. This guide is written to help taxpayers filing FY 2025-26 income returns in AY 2026-27 avoid exactly those traps.

Read this as a structured review note, not as a guarantee of refund speed or notice avoidance. The aim is to help you organise the facts, pick the right route, and recognise when a CA's review adds value.

## Quick reference summary

| Area | Practical filing decision |
| --- | --- |
| Assessment year | Use AY 2026-27 for income earned in FY 2025-26. |
| Form selection | Start with ITR-1 only when the facts fit; move to ITR-2, ITR-3, or ITR-4 when income heads or eligibility require it. |
| Evidence | Keep Form 16, Form 16A, AIS, TIS, Form 26AS, bank records, broker statements, challans, and computation notes together. |
| Review point | Use CA review where there is capital gains, business income, foreign assets, tax-credit mismatch, refund risk, or notice history. |

## The AY 2026-27 context

This is a transition-heavy filing season. References to the Income Tax Act, 2025 and Tax Year terminology are circulating widely, yet returns for FY 2025-26 income continue to be filed for AY 2026-27 under the Income Tax Act, 1961 framework. The practical risk: taxpayers may conflate the filing year, payment law, ITR form, and document trail.

A safer mental model is to treat the return as a reconciliation project. Every income head, deduction, tax paid, TDS credit, refund or demand, and disclosure needs a supporting document. The portal prefill is a useful starting point — not a final answer. A tax calculator helps estimate the liability, but it is not the return. Social media tips can surface the right question, but they cannot substitute for the taxpayer's actual Form 16, AIS, Form 26AS, broker report, business books, or foreign asset statement.

## Where to begin: a six-step workflow

- Confirm the income belongs to FY 2025-26 and the return covers AY 2026-27.
- List every income head: salary, house property, capital gains, business or profession, other sources, foreign income, and exempt income.
- Download Form 16 or Form 16A, AIS, TIS, Form 26AS, and challan records before final computation.
- Choose the ITR form after reviewing exclusions — not before.
- Compare old and new regimes where the taxpayer has salary, deductions, HRA, home-loan interest, or business/profession constraints.
- Review refund position, tax-credit mismatch, notice risk, and e-verification status before treating the filing as complete.

## What the official guidance says

The Income Tax Department lists ITR-1, ITR-2, ITR-3, and ITR-4 applicability for AY 2026-27. ITR-1 and ITR-4 have specific limits and exclusions; ITR-2 covers non-business cases that fall outside ITR-1's scope; ITR-3 covers business or profession income. Always treat the official portal material as the authoritative rule source.

## Choosing between the four forms

| Form | Use only when the facts fit |
| --- | --- |
| ITR-1 | Resident individual, within eligible income limits, with salary or pension, house property, other sources, agricultural income within the limit, and eligible limited section 112A LTCG where allowed. |
| ITR-2 | Individual or HUF without business or profession income, but with capital gains, foreign assets, multiple income categories, or other ITR-1 exclusions. |
| ITR-3 | Individual or HUF with business or profession income, including trading, F&O, freelance, or proprietorship cases. |
| ITR-4 | Eligible resident individual, HUF, or firm other than LLP using presumptive taxation under applicable sections, subject to form exclusions and limits. |

The critical discipline is to decide the form after the documents are visible. A salaried taxpayer may look eligible for ITR-1 right up until the broker report shows capital gains, AIS flags a foreign dividend, or a carried-forward loss needs to be disclosed. Similarly, a freelancer may seem straightforward until receipts, TDS, GST turnover, books, or presumptive-tax eligibility point toward ITR-3 or ITR-4.

## Three real-world examples

**Routine salary case.** One employer, Form 16, bank interest, no capital gains or foreign assets. Start with Form 16, compare AIS and Form 26AS. If salary, TDS, interest, and bank account details align, compare regimes and select the appropriate form. The typical mistake here is filing only from Form 16 and overlooking bank interest in AIS. The tax gap may be small, but a mismatch can stall a refund or trigger follow-up questions.

**Salary plus investment case.** A taxpayer with salary and equity mutual fund redemptions cannot jump straight to the easiest form. The relevant question is whether the gains fit within simplified-form limits and whether there are losses, multiple transactions, or disclosure requirements that point elsewhere. Run through the broker statement, AIS securities data, and capital gains computation before deciding. Filing a simpler form when losses need to be carried forward or item-wise reporting is required can create a defective return.

**Freelancer with Form 16A.** Form 16A proves TDS was deducted; it does not settle the ITR form question. The taxpayer still needs invoices, bank credits, expense records, GST linkage where relevant, and a view on whether presumptive taxation is available and desirable. If presumptive treatment does not fit, ITR-3 is likely the right route. The common error is claiming the TDS refund without properly reporting the gross income that generated the deduction.

## Document reconciliation checklist

| Document | What to match | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form 16 | Salary, allowances, deductions, TDS, employer details | Builds the salary schedule and helps compare old vs new regime. |
| Form 16A | Non-salary TDS, deductor TAN, income nature | Refund claims should report both income and matching TDS. |
| AIS/TIS | Reported income and transactions | Helps identify interest, dividends, securities, rent, foreign income, and mismatch risk. |
| Form 26AS | Tax credits, TDS/TCS, challans, refund or demand data | Tax credit claims should match portal records where possible. |
| Broker statement | Sale value, cost, holding period, STT, gains or losses | Supports ITR-2 or ITR-3 capital gains and trading classification. |
| Bank statement | Interest, refunds, tax payments, business receipts | Supports other income, refund-bank validation, and cash-flow checks. |

## Situations that call for a pause before selecting the form

The safest selection method is exclusion-first. Rather than asking whether ITR-1 or ITR-4 is convenient, ask whether any fact disqualifies the taxpayer from the simpler form. Capital gains, foreign assets, business income, carried-forward losses, multiple house properties, director or unlisted-equity disclosures, and non-resident status can all push the return toward a different form.

Mixed employment and investment activity is another area that trips people up. Many salaried individuals sell shares, mutual funds, ESOPs, RSUs, or crypto during the year. Some limited situations may still fit a simplified form depending on current utility support and the specific facts — but losses, foreign assets, multiple schedules, and transaction detail often require ITR-2 or ITR-3.

Professional or freelance income deserves its own check. Form 16A proves TDS; it does not confirm ITR-4 eligibility. Activity type, receipts, presumptive-tax eligibility, expenses, GST data, and whether books or audit implications arise all need to be reviewed. When the income is business or profession income and presumptive treatment does not fit, ITR-3 is the safer choice.

| Situation | Likely review direction |
| --- | --- |
| Salary plus FD interest | Start with ITR-1 eligibility, then check AIS and Form 26AS. |
| Salary plus equity gains | Check ITR-2 or permitted simplified treatment only after reviewing gains and losses. |
| Freelancer with Form 16A | Check ITR-3 versus ITR-4 based on eligibility and records. |
| F&O or intraday trading | Review business-income classification, books, losses, and due-date impact. |
| Foreign assets or NRI/RNOR facts | Review residential status, Schedule FA, foreign tax credit, and ITR-2/ITR-3 needs. |

When in doubt, write a short note explaining why a particular form was chosen. That note should reference the documents reviewed and the exclusions considered — it helps during CA review and prevents form selection from becoming guesswork.

## Before filing: an internal review checklist

- The return uses AY 2026-27 and not Tax Year 2026-27.
- The chosen ITR form supports every income head and schedule in the file.
- The tax regime is legally available and matches the deduction treatment.
- AIS, TIS, Form 26AS, Form 16, Form 16A, and challans have been reconciled.
- Any mismatch has an action: wait, file AIS feedback, ask deductor correction, revise, rectify, or respond to notice.
- The final preview shows correct PAN, bank account, filing section, refund or demand, and e-verification plan.

## Common filing mistakes

- Defaulting to ITR-1 because salary is the primary income stream.
- Overlooking business or profession income that requires a different form.
- Missing foreign asset disclosure obligations.
- Using ITR-4 without first verifying presumptive-tax eligibility.

The costliest mistake is usually selecting the wrong route altogether. A revised return, rectification request, AIS feedback, ITR-U, demand payment, grievance, and notice reply each address different problems. Do not default to whichever option is most visible on the portal.

## Preparing a reviewer handoff note

Before submission, put together a one-page handoff note that a second reviewer can follow without opening every attachment. It should state the taxpayer profile, selected ITR form, selected regime, major income heads, documents reviewed, unresolved mismatches, and the reason the filing route was chosen. This becomes invaluable if the return later needs revision, rectification, refund follow-up, or a notice response.

Keep the note factual and grounded. It should not claim the filing is risk-free. It should record what was checked, what was assumed, and what still depends on Income Tax Department processing, deductor corrections, bank validation, or taxpayer-provided records. When a figure comes from AIS, Form 26AS, Form 16, a broker statement, a challan, or a bank certificate, name that source next to the figure. Keep everything together in the same folder as the computation, acknowledgement, source downloads, and proof.

## When waiting is better than filing

Rushing has a cost. Waiting for cleaner data makes sense when TDS credits are still incomplete, Form 16 has not been issued, AIS is still being updated, the employer or bank has not corrected a statement, a broker report is missing, or a large refund depends on records being settled. Waiting in these situations is not procrastination — it prevents an unsupported claim.

That said, do not wait passively. If the return involves a deadline-sensitive loss, an old-regime choice, a notice response, or an audit implication, record what is missing and who needs to fix it, then set a date.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ITR-1 always for salaried taxpayers?

No. It is only for eligible resident individuals within the form conditions and exclusions.

### Which form is used for business income?

ITR-3 is common for business or profession income unless an eligible presumptive-tax ITR-4 route applies.

### Should I get CA review before filing?

Use CA review when the facts are not routine, when there is refund or notice risk, or when the return includes capital gains, trading income, foreign assets, business income, regime changes, or AIS/TDS mismatch.

## CA technical review note

For this topic, the reviewer should document the selected assessment year, taxpayer status, ITR form, income head, tax regime, source records, and the reason each major figure appears in the return. If the position depends on timing — Form 16 issue, AIS updates, TDS return processing, e-verification, revised-return deadline, or notice response window — write the date next to the decision.

The minimum file should include the computation, portal downloads, source statements, challans, acknowledgement, and correspondence. This article should not be read as legal advice for a specific taxpayer without reviewing that taxpayer's documents.

## Final takeaway

AY 2026-27 filing should be calm, documented, and route-aware. Choose the assessment year first, then the form, then the regime, then the schedules, then the correction or filing path. For ordinary facts, this framework keeps the return clean. For complex or high-value cases, get the treatment reviewed before filing.
