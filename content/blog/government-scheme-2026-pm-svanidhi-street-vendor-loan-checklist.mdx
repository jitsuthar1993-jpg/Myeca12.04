# PM SVANidhi Loan Checklist for Street Vendors

A PM SVANidhi request should connect the vendor certificate or local-body reference, applicant identity, bank account, mobile, borrowing, and repayment record. Preserve later digital-payment or repayment evidence separately from the original loan application.

Separate vendor recognition from the lender's credit and repayment records. The local-body or recommendation trail should identify the vending activity and applicant, while the lender file should show application, sanction or rejection, disbursement, repayment, and any later support entry. A bank credit cannot repair a missing vendor record, and a vendor certificate does not prove repayment. Keep disputes with the correct authority instead of combining them into one general follow-up.

## Define the vendor activity and borrowing request separately

A PM SVANidhi request should connect a real street-vending activity to a specific credit need. Begin with the vendor certificate, local-body reference, or recognised recommendation route applicable to the applicant. Then describe the vending activity, location context, applicant identity, mobile number, bank account, amount sought, intended business use, existing borrowing, and repayment position. Vendor recognition supports the activity record; it does not itself establish a lender's credit decision or later repayment.

Review the local-body and bank details for consistency before applying. Do not invent vending history, location, turnover, digital-payment activity, or repayment evidence. If vendor recognition is missing or wrong, address that route first rather than expecting the lender to repair it. If the borrowing request is unclear, explain the business use before treating the application as ready.

## Route vendor recognition and credit issues to different owners

The vendor owns the accuracy of activity and application information. The local body or recognised vendor route controls certification or recommendation records. The lender controls credit appraisal, sanction or rejection, disbursement, loan account, and repayment. Payment-service or bank records control their transactions. A vendor certificate cannot prove repayment, and a bank credit cannot repair a missing or incorrect local-body record.

Preserve the vendor reference, submitted loan request, lender questions, decision, disbursement, repayment entries, and later digital-payment or support records separately. If a source record changes, retain the old version and acknowledgement. Send a vendor-status dispute to the local route and a loan-account dispute to the lender. Avoid combining every issue into one general grievance, because each owner needs the reference for the stage it controls.

## Follow recognition, loan, and repayment as linked timelines

Build the chronology from vendor recognition or recommendation through loan submission, lender appraisal, decision, disbursement, repayment periods, and any later programme-linked entry. For a pending application, identify whether local verification or lender review is outstanding. For a repayment issue, cite the loan account, due period, payment reference, and lender response rather than relying on the original vendor certificate.

Keep digital-payment activity and later borrowing evidence in their actual periods without rewriting the first application. If the vendor changes bank, mobile, or vending details, document when each owner recognised the update. The completed file should show who established the vending activity, how the lender assessed the request, what was disbursed, and how repayment progressed without presenting vendor recognition as a loan approval or a later transaction as proof of the original facts.

Before seeking a later loan stage or support entry, reconcile the vendor's recognised activity, loan account, repayment schedule, actual payments, and any digital-payment record the route asks to consider. Note gaps and obtain the relevant owner's response rather than creating missing transaction evidence. If the vending location or activity changes, update the local-body record through its route and explain the change to the lender where relevant. This keeps later borrowing decisions grounded in a traceable vendor and repayment history.

## Build the vendor and repayment trail

Connect the applicant to the vending activity through the vendor certificate, local-body reference, survey or recommendation route where applicable, identity, mobile, and bank account. The application should describe the same person and activity across each record. Preserve the local-body interaction where the vendor record needs correction or confirmation.

After a loan is sanctioned, keep disbursement, repayment, digital-payment, and later support records separate from the original eligibility file. A bank credit proves that money moved; it does not by itself explain vendor status, repayment performance, or a later programme benefit. Review failed debits, account changes, or inconsistent mobile details through the official channel.

Retain the submitted request, acknowledgement, lender communication, sanction or rejection, and repayment evidence. Avoid unofficial promises of approval or fee-based shortcuts. Escalate a missing vendor record, disputed local verification, wrong bank account, duplicate application, or repayment entry that the lender cannot reconcile.
