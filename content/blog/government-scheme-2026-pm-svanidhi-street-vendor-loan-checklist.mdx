# PM SVANidhi Loan Checklist for Street Vendors

PM SVANidhi — the PM Street Vendor's AtmaNirbhar Nidhi scheme — provides collateral-free working capital loans to street vendors who were operating before March 2020. The loan starts at ₹10,000 and can scale up to ₹50,000 in subsequent tranches, depending on repayment behaviour and digital transaction history. Interest subsidy is also available on timely repayment. For vendors who have never had a formal bank relationship, this scheme is often their first structured credit experience.

This guide explains what documents you need, what to check before applying, and where to verify the official process. It does not promise loan sanction, disbursement timelines, or any specific interest rate.

## Who is searching for this

Street vendors searching for PM SVANidhi loan documents in 2026 are typically vendors who have heard about the scheme through a municipal office, a local NGO, or a fellow vendor. Their main questions are practical: do I need a vending certificate, which bank should I approach, what if my Aadhaar address is different from where I vend now, and will my loan history affect a higher-tranche application.

The scheme operates through Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) and lending institutions. The first step is confirming your vendor status through the ULB — either through a vending certificate already issued, or through a letter of recommendation if the survey process is still pending. This document is the anchor of the application; without it, or with a mismatch in the name, further steps become complicated.

## Quick checklist

- Confirm your vendor registration status with the relevant Urban Local Body before applying.
- Ensure your Aadhaar is linked to an active bank account in your name — loan disbursement happens through this account.
- If you have an existing loan under PM SVANidhi and are applying for a higher tranche, check that repayment records are reflected accurately.
- Keep a record of digital transactions if you want to strengthen your case for higher-tranche eligibility.
- Do not rely on intermediaries who charge fees; the application through the official PM SVANidhi portal is free.

## Documents to keep ready

| Document | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| vendor certificate or reference | Issued by the ULB; confirms that you are a recognised street vendor. Without this, or with name discrepancies, the application may not proceed. |
| bank account | Must be in the vendor's name and linked to Aadhaar; loan is disbursed directly to this account. |
| mobile number | Active number is needed for OTP-based portal registration and for receiving transaction alerts. |
| KYC | Aadhaar serves as the primary KYC document; all name and address details must be consistent across records. |
| PAN and bank details | Needed for identity matching; also relevant for any AY 2026-27 tax filing if business income is being reported. |
| A short review note | Documents what was verified, which source was checked, and what corrections are pending. |

## Practical example

A vegetable vendor in Lucknow wants to apply for a ₹10,000 SVANidhi loan. He has a vending certificate from the ULB but his bank account was opened years ago in a different name spelling — "Mohammad Rashid" on the bank account versus "Mohd. Rashid" on Aadhaar. The bank branch flags this during KYC verification, and the loan application is put on hold.

He corrects the bank account name to match Aadhaar, resubmits the KYC, and the application moves forward. The two-week delay could have been avoided had he compared his bank passbook and Aadhaar before starting the process. This is the most common friction point in SVANidhi applications — not missing documents, but inconsistent details across the documents that exist.

## Review notes for street vendors

The reviewer should check: whether the ULB vendor certificate is in the applicant's name and matches Aadhaar, whether the bank account is active and KYC-compliant, whether any prior SVANidhi tranche repayment records are clean, and whether the mobile number is registered and functional. If the application also has AY 2026-27 ITR implications — for instance, if the vendor is reporting business income — note the income head, the ITR form (typically ITR-4 for presumptive income), the tax regime, and TDS or TCS credit status. For the scheme portion, record the ULB reference, the lending institution, and the application number.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is PM SVANidhi eligibility guaranteed by this guide?

No. Eligibility depends on the official portal, current scheme rules, state or ministry verification, and the applicant's documents.

### Should I use only social media information before applying?

No. Use social posts only to identify the issue, then verify the rule and application status on official government sources.

### Why keep tax records for a government scheme?

Many applications ask for income, bank, identity, or business records. A clean document trail reduces avoidable mismatch and follow-up questions.

## Final takeaway

PM SVANidhi is a well-designed first-credit opportunity for vendors who have operated informally for years. The documents required are not complicated, but they need to be consistent with each other. Get your ULB certificate, confirm your Aadhaar-bank linkage, and apply through the official portal. If your business income needs to be reported in your AY 2026-27 ITR, speak to a CA about the right form and regime before filing.
