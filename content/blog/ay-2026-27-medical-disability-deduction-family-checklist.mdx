# AY 2026-27 Medical Disability Deduction Checklist for Families

Medical and disability deductions are among the most document-intensive claims in an individual's ITR. The rules under Section 80D (health insurance premiums and preventive health check-ups), Section 80DD (maintenance and medical treatment of a disabled dependent), and Section 80U (self-disability) have distinct eligibility conditions — and the deduction is only as strong as the paperwork behind it. This checklist is for FY 2025-26 income being filed for AY 2026-27.

## Where families go wrong

The most common gap is not the deduction amount — it is the supporting documents. A certificate from a competent medical authority is a hard requirement for Section 80DD and Section 80U claims. Without it, the deduction is indefensible during scrutiny. Similarly, for Section 80D, insurers sometimes issue combined premium receipts that do not segregate the self premium from the senior-citizen parent premium, making it impossible to apply the correct limits.

Two things to confirm before filing: first, are you on the old tax regime? Section 80D, 80DD, and 80U deductions are not available under the new tax regime. Second, have payments been made in a mode other than cash (as required by the Act) for Section 80D claims? Cash payments for health insurance premiums are not eligible.

## Pre-filing checklist

- Confirm you are filing under the old tax regime — medical and disability deductions do not apply under the new regime.
- Obtain original receipts for health insurance premiums paid; the insurer's official receipt or policy schedule is stronger than a payment gateway record.
- For Section 80DD or Section 80U, ensure the disability certificate from a competent medical authority is current and covers FY 2025-26.
- Note the dependent's details — name, relationship, and whether they are fully dependent on you.
- Keep payment proofs (bank transfer or cheque), not cash receipts, for premium claims.
- Cross-check the AIS and Form 26AS, and run a reconciliation before finalising the ITR.

## A practical scenario

A taxpayer's elderly parent is being treated for a chronic condition. The taxpayer has paid health insurance premiums for the parent and also made direct medical payments during FY 2025-26. The premium payments can support a Section 80D claim; direct medical expenses for a specified disease may qualify under Section 80DDB if a specialist certificate is obtained. Both claims require different documents, different limits, and the old regime to be applicable. Bundling them without segregating the amounts or the certificates is what leads to notices.

Take each deduction separately, verify the applicable limit for the relevant section, and document it independently. That discipline is the difference between a clean assessment and a demand notice.

## Reviewer guidance

Confirm the tax regime in use before examining any deduction claim. Then check: which section applies (80D, 80DD, 80U, or 80DDB), what supporting documents were seen, whether the dependent relationship and disability certification are in order, and whether any payment was made in cash (which would disqualify a premium claim). Note unresolved gaps and the recommended next step.

## Bottom line

Medical and disability deductions reward careful paperwork. Confirm the regime, collect the right certificates, segregate each section's claim, and keep everything in a folder you can revisit if the return is picked up for scrutiny.
