# AY 2026-27 Donation Deduction Record Guide for 80G and 80GGC

If you gave to a charitable trust or a political party during FY 2025-26 and now want to claim the deduction under Section 80G or 80GGC, the rule itself is rarely the hard part. The real work is proof: lining up your donation receipts, the donee's registration details, the mode of payment, and old-regime eligibility so that everything tells the same story. This guide walks through that record-keeping for income earned in FY 2025-26 and reported in AY 2026-27. Treat it as a cautious filing checklist. It does not promise a refund, a processing timeline, or anything that replaces a look at the official utility.

## Who this guide is for

People who reach a topic like this usually already know the headline. They have heard of 80G or 80GGC. What they are unsure about is the paperwork, which portal to trust, how it ties back to their tax records, and what to actually do next. The one habit worth building early: never paste an answer you found somewhere into a return or application. Begin with the official source, then assemble a document file that someone can review later.

For MyeCA readers, three points carry most of the weight. Keep the assessment-year or scheme-year context straight so you are not mixing periods. Reconcile your name, PAN, Aadhaar, bank account, and income records before anything is submitted. And hold on to every acknowledgement or application reference, because chasing a refund, a notice, or a follow-up later is far harder once that number is lost.

## Quick checklist

- Confirm the official portal or Income Tax Department source before acting.
- Keep identity, bank, income, and scheme-specific documents in one folder.
- Match AIS, Form 26AS, Form 16, Form 16A, GST, or bank records where they apply.
- Do not assume eligibility, refund, subsidy, or loan approval without official verification.
- Use a CA or expert review where the record affects tax, business compliance, or high-value benefits.

## A practical example

Picture someone who searches "donation deduction 80G 80GGC AY 2026-27" after spotting a portal message, an employer record, a bank credit, or a requirement on some application. The tempting move is to file or apply straight away. The better move is to gather the documents first, open the official source links below, and jot down a short note setting out the facts. The moment a name, bank, Aadhaar, PAN, income, or TDS detail does not line up, stop and fix that mismatch before you lean on the record.

Yes, this is slower than copying a ready-made answer. It is also a lot safer. In practice, most tax and scheme trouble does not come from getting the main rule wrong. It comes from small details that disagree with each other across documents.

## What the reviewer should capture

For donors claiming deductions, the reviewer should pin down five things: the user profile, the official source checked, the documents actually seen, any mismatch still open, and the next action. Where the file feeds into AY 2026-27 ITR filing, add a separate line for the income head, the ITR form, the tax regime, the TDS or TCS credit, and the e-verification status. Where the file relates to a government scheme, note the scheme portal, the application reference, the eligibility documents, and whether the bank account is ready to receive a credit.
