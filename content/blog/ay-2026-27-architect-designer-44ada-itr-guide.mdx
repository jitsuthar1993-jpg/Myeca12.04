# AY 2026-27 ITR Guide for Architects and Designers Under 44ADA

If you run an architecture or design practice on your own, the recurring question at filing time is a simple one: can you use presumptive taxation for professionals, and does ITR-4 or ITR-3 actually suit your situation? This guide works through that for FY 2025-26 income, which is what you report in AY 2026-27. Treat it as a careful, conservative checklist. It does not promise a refund, predict how long processing takes, or replace a look at the live filing utility on the portal.

## Who this is for

The people landing on this topic are usually architects and designers who recognise the broad label but get stuck on the detail: which documents to gather, which official portal to trust, which tax records to reconcile, and what to do next. The one habit worth keeping is to never paste a stray answer from the internet straight into your return or an application. Begin with official sources, then assemble a document file that someone can review later.

For MyeCA readers, that boils down to three points. Keep the assessment-year or scheme-year context straight from the start. Reconcile your name, PAN, Aadhaar, bank account, and income figures before you submit anything. And hold on to every acknowledgement or application reference, because any later refund, subsidy, loan, or notice gets much harder to handle without it.

## Quick checklist

- Confirm the official portal or Income Tax Department source before acting.
- Keep identity, bank, income, and scheme-specific documents in one folder.
- Match AIS, Form 26AS, Form 16, Form 16A, GST, or bank records where they apply.
- Do not assume eligibility, refund, subsidy, or loan approval without official verification.
- Use a CA or expert review where the record affects tax, business compliance, or high-value benefits.

## A worked example

Picture a designer who searches for "44ADA architect ITR AY 2026-27" after spotting a portal message, an employer record, a bank credit, or a requirement attached to an application. Rather than rushing to file or apply, they pull together the documents, open the official source links listed below, and jot down a short note setting out the facts. The moment a name, bank, Aadhaar, PAN, income, or TDS mismatch surfaces, they stop and sort it out before treating the record as reliable.

Yes, this is slower than lifting a generic answer off a search result. It is also far safer. A surprising share of tax and scheme trouble has nothing to do with the headline rule; it traces back to small details that do not agree across documents.

## Review notes for architects and designers

A reviewer should pin down the user profile, the official source that was checked, the documents seen, any unresolved mismatch, and the next action. Where the file feeds AY 2026-27 ITR filing, the note should additionally record the income head, the ITR form, the tax regime, the TDS or TCS credit, and the e-verification status. Where a government scheme is involved instead, the note should set out the scheme portal, the application reference, the eligibility documents, and whether the file is ready for a bank credit.
