# AY 2026-27 NRI India Rent and TDS ITR Guide

An NRI receiving rent from property in India needs to report the property income and match tenant tax deduction with the credit actually available under the owner’s PAN. The rent agreement, bank receipts, tenant TDS filings, municipal-tax evidence, and loan-interest certificate answer different parts of that return.

The most common problem is not the rent calculation alone. It is a broken trail between the tenant’s gross payment, tax deducted, net amount received, and Form 26AS credit.

## Confirm the property and ownership facts

List each Indian property separately. Record the ownership share, co-owner details, rental period, tenant, monthly rent, security deposit terms, and whether the property was vacant for part of the year. The return treatment may change where ownership is shared, the property has mixed use, or rent relates to more than one financial year.

Keep the registered or executed rent agreement with title or ownership records. Bank credits help prove receipt timing, but they do not replace the agreement or decide the taxable annual value by themselves.

## Match tenant deduction with the rent ledger

Rent paid to a non-resident can create withholding obligations for the tenant under the applicable non-resident payment rules. The tenant’s certificate and Form 26AS should be matched to the same payer, property period, and gross rent recorded by the owner.

Prepare a month-wise working:

| Month | Gross rent due | Tax deducted by tenant | Net amount received | Form 26AS credit status |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Each rental month | Agreement or invoice amount | Tenant certificate or challan detail | NRO or other receiving-account credit | Matched, missing, or different |

Do not report only the net bank receipt as rental income. Conversely, do not claim a tax credit merely because the tenant says tax was deducted. The credit claim needs to be supported by the taxpayer’s records and the tax-credit statement.

## Compute house-property income from the right records

The rental-income computation should separately consider gross annual value, municipal taxes paid by the owner where eligible, the statutory deduction, and interest treatment where applicable. A home-loan certificate should be connected to the same property and period; the certificate does not prove ownership, property use, or every condition relevant to the deduction.

Where there are co-owners, split rent, municipal tax, interest, and TDS credit using the supported ownership and payment facts. A tenant certificate issued entirely to one PAN can require correction when the underlying ownership and rent arrangement differs.

Use the [income tax calculator](/calculators/income-tax) only as an estimate aid. The house-property schedule still needs the property-level figures and correct return form.

## Handle a missing or incorrect TDS credit

If the tenant deducted tax but the credit is absent or incorrect in Form 26AS, compare the tenant’s certificate, challan or filing details, PAN, payment dates, and gross rent. Ask the tenant or deductor to amend an inaccurate source filing. Retain the correspondence and do not silently reduce gross rent to make the return match the available credit.

The Income Tax Department’s [tax-credit mismatch FAQ](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/node/11487) describes the portal-level mismatch issue. AIS can provide another information view, but Form 26AS and the underlying deduction records remain central to the credit claim.

## Check NRI-specific return questions

Residential status must be determined for the relevant year; an NRO account label does not establish tax residence. Also check whether the taxpayer has other Indian income, capital gains, foreign reporting obligations, treaty questions, or losses that change the return form or schedules.

Use the [NRI and RNOR status guide](/blog/nri-rnor-resident-status-itr-ay-2026-27) for the residence analysis and the [ITR-2 checklist](/blog/itr-2-checklist-capital-gains-foreign-assets-ay-2026-27) where the return includes gains or foreign-asset questions.

## Filing checklist for the landlord

1. Prepare a property-wise rent and vacancy working.
2. Match gross rent, tenant deduction, net bank receipt, AIS, and Form 26AS.
3. Verify municipal-tax payment and property-linked interest evidence.
4. Confirm ownership shares and the return form from the taxpayer’s full facts.
5. Review refund-bank validation before claiming a refund.
6. E-verify the filed return and retain the computation, tenant records, tax-credit statements, and acknowledgement.

Where the tenant’s filing, ownership share, or credit amount remains disputed, resolve that issue before submission or obtain assistance using the complete property and TDS records.
