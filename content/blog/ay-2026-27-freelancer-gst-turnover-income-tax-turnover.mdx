# AY 2026-27 GST Turnover vs Income Tax Turnover for Freelancers

GST turnover, invoice value, TDS-reported gross receipts, and bank collections can differ for legitimate reasons such as tax components, credit notes, timing, advances, and withholding. They still need a documented bridge.

Build an invoice register with fee, GST, credit note, TDS, collection date, and amount outstanding. Compare that register with GSTR summaries, Form 16A, AIS, Form 26AS, and bank receipts.

## Explain why the freelancer's four annual totals differ

Build an invoice register that separates fee before GST, GST charged, credit notes, reimbursements, TDS, platform fees, collection date, and outstanding balance. From that register, calculate distinct totals for GST reporting, professional income review, TDS-reported gross amounts, and bank collections. Differences may arise from timing, tax components, advances, refunds, or withholding, but every material difference should trace to a client or invoice instead of being explained only by a year-end plug.

Use the reconciled activity to decide the income-tax treatment and form. Keep foreign-client receipts, mixed business activities, and money collected for another person outside the ordinary domestic-fee bridge until their facts are settled. If a client reported the wrong TDS amount or PAN, preserve the correction request rather than changing the invoice total to match Form 26AS. Likewise, do not alter income-tax receipts merely to imitate a GST summary. Pause when invoices cannot be connected to the same service trail, when personal and business receipts share an unexplained account, or when the proposed presumptive approach does not fit the freelancer's actual work.

## Read GSTR summaries, invoice register, and bank statement for different facts

- **GSTR summaries:** GSTR summaries records the indirect-tax view of GST turnover income tax turnover freelancer. Bridge invoice value, tax, credit notes, timing, and registration scope to invoice register before using any turnover figure in the income-tax return.
- **Invoice register:** Invoice register should identify the customer, service or supply, gross amount, tax component, and date relevant to the proposed return treatment. Reconcile cancellations, credit notes, withholding, and collections with bank statement.
- **Bank statement:** Bank statement proves the date and net movement of money relevant to the proposed return treatment; it rarely proves the whole tax treatment. Connect each material credit or debit to correction trail and filing acknowledgement and explain transfers, withholding, or non-income amounts.

## Resolve GSTR summaries and invoice register differences before filing

Pause for mixed activities, foreign accounts, unsupported expenses, unexplained turnover differences, or invoices and GST returns that cannot be connected to the same service trail.

Before submitting, understand why GST turnover and income-tax receipts can differ and how to document it. Record what bank statement establishes, explain any remaining difference, and retain the correction trail and filing acknowledgement with the final computation.

## Reconcile four different freelancer totals

Prepare an invoice register showing the fee before GST, GST amount, credit notes, TDS, platform charges, collection date, and amount outstanding. Compare that register with GST returns, Form 16A, Form 26AS, AIS, and bank receipts. GST turnover, income-tax receipts, TDS-reported gross amounts, and cash collections can differ for explainable reasons; forcing them into one number hides the issue.

Name each difference as timing, tax component, advance, refund, credit note, unpaid invoice, foreign receipt, or reporting error. Keep net bank credits separate from gross fees and withholding. If a payer reports the wrong amount or PAN, request a correction and retain the response.

Use the reconciled activity to review return form, presumptive eligibility, books, expenses, GST filing, and tax credits. Preserve invoices, contracts, GST records, TDS certificates, bank statements, the bridge between totals, computation, and acknowledgement. Escalate mixed activities, foreign accounts, unsupported expenses, or turnover differences that remain unexplained.
