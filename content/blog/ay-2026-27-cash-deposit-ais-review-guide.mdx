# AY 2026-27 Cash Deposit AIS Review Guide

A cash entry showing up in your Annual Information Statement tends to set off the same worry for everyone: can I explain where this money came from? That is the real task here, and this guide walks through it. We are dealing with FY 2025-26 income that you will report for AY 2026-27. Read it as a cautious, step-by-step checklist for pinning each deposit to a source document, not as a promise about refunds, processing times, or a stand-in for whatever the official utility actually shows on your screen.

## Who lands on this page

Almost everyone who searches this out has already spotted a cash deposit in AIS and recognises the broad heading. What trips them up is the detail: which documents back it, which portal to trust, how their tax records line up, and what to do next. The one habit worth avoiding is pasting some stranger's answer straight into a return or an application. Begin with the official sources, then assemble a file that a reviewer can actually look through.

For MyeCA readers, that boils down to three habits. Keep the relevant year clear, whether it is an assessment year or a scheme year. Cross-check your name, PAN, Aadhaar, bank account, and income figures before you submit anything. And hold on to the acknowledgement or application reference, because any later work on a refund, subsidy, loan, or notice gets much harder once that number is lost.

## Quick checklist

- Confirm the official portal or Income Tax Department source before acting.
- Keep identity, bank, income, and scheme-specific documents in one folder.
- Match AIS, Form 26AS, Form 16, Form 16A, GST, or bank records where they apply.
- Do not assume eligibility, refund, subsidy, or loan approval without official verification.
- Use a CA or expert review where the record affects tax, business compliance, or high-value benefits.

## A worked example

Picture someone searching for "cash deposit AIS ITR AY 2026-27" after a portal message, an employer entry, a bank credit, or an application form prompts the question. Rather than rushing to file or apply, they pull together the supporting documents, open the official links listed below, and jot down a short note that lays out the facts. The moment a name, bank, Aadhaar, PAN, income, or TDS detail fails to match, they stop, fix that gap, and only then treat the record as reliable.

Yes, this is slower than lifting a one-line answer off the internet. It is also far less likely to blow up later. In practice, most tax and scheme headaches do not come from the headline rule at all. They come from small details that disagree with each other across documents.

## What a reviewer should capture

A reviewer working a cash-deposit file should record the user profile, the official source checked, the documents seen, any mismatch still open, and the next action. Where the file feeds into AY 2026-27 ITR filing, note separately the income head, the ITR form, the tax regime, the TDS or TCS credit, and the e-verification status. Where it instead relates to a government scheme, note separately the scheme portal, the application reference, the eligibility documents, and whether the bank credit is ready to go.
