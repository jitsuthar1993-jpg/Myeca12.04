# AICTE Pragati and Saksham Scholarship Readiness Guide

AICTE runs two distinct scholarship programmes for technical education students: Pragati, aimed at girl students pursuing degree or diploma courses in AICTE-approved institutions, and Saksham, designed for students with disabilities enrolled in similar programmes. Both are applied for through the National Scholarship Portal, and both have specific requirements around family income, institute recognition, and year of study that students often underestimate until they are mid-application.

This guide is about getting your documents and records in order before the portal opens. It does not guarantee selection or disbursement timing.

## What applicants commonly miss

The two problems that derail otherwise eligible applications are mismatched income certificates and bank accounts that do not match the applicant's name. For Pragati and Saksham, the income certificate must reflect the family's current financial position and be issued by a competent authority — a rough figure or an old document from a previous year's application is unlikely to be accepted without question.

Similarly, the bank account linked to the application must be in the student's own name, and the name on the account must match the name on the admission documents exactly. A single-character difference between how a name appears on a marksheet versus a bank passbook has caused disbursement failures in past cycles.

## Institute recognition: a step that trips up many students

Pragati and Saksham apply only to students in AICTE-approved institutions. Before filling the application, look up your institution's approval status on the AICTE portal directly — do not rely on information the college gave you or on older circulars. Approvals can change, and an unapproved status at the time of application will make your application ineligible regardless of how strong your other documents are.

## A practical scenario

A second-year engineering student searches for "AICTE Pragati Saksham scholarship 2026" in early June after her college notice board mentions the application window. She finds the National Scholarship Portal link, starts the form, and gets stuck at the income field — her family's income certificate was issued eighteen months ago for a different application. The portal flags it.

Rather than guess at a figure, she pauses, gets a fresh income certificate from the tehsildar's office, and re-enters the application. Her bank account name matched, her institution was AICTE-approved, and the rest of her documents were in order. The application went through cleanly.

The lesson: the income certificate is often the longest-lead-time document. Arrange it first, not last.

## For the CA or advisor reviewing this file

Check that the family income figure used in the application is consistent with what is declared in the ITR for the relevant assessment year. If a parent has filed an AY 2026-27 return, verify the total income figure there matches or is reconcilable with the income certificate submitted to the scholarship portal. Where the applicant's own bank account is used for disbursement, confirm no TDS or unexplained credits appear that could complicate future filings.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is AICTE Pragati and Saksham eligibility guaranteed by this guide?

No. Eligibility depends on the official portal, current scheme rules, state or ministry verification, and the applicant's documents.

### Should I use only social media information before applying?

No. Use social posts only to identify the issue, then verify the rule and application status on official government sources.

### Why keep tax records for a government scheme?

Many applications ask for income, bank, identity, or business records. A clean document trail reduces avoidable mismatch and follow-up questions.

## Summing up

Pragati and Saksham are well-structured scholarships with clear eligibility criteria. The applications that fail usually do so on document details — stale income certificates, mismatched names, or institutions whose AICTE approval was not verified. Get those three things right, and the rest of the process is relatively straightforward.
