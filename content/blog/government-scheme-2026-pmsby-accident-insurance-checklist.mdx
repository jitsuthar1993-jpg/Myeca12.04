# PMSBY Accident Insurance Checklist for Workers and Families

Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY) is an accidental death and disability cover offered at a very low annual premium through savings bank accounts. It provides ₹2 lakh for accidental death or total permanent disability, and ₹1 lakh for partial permanent disability. The policy year runs from 1 June to 31 May, and the premium is auto-debited each year around 1 June. For workers and their families, the scheme is straightforward in principle — but lapse due to insufficient balance, wrong nominee details, or a closed account can make it unavailable precisely when it is needed most. This checklist helps you verify that your PMSBY cover is in order for 2026.

## Who is looking for this checklist

Workers in informal employment, daily wage earners, gig workers, and small-business owners are the primary subscribers of PMSBY. Because the premium is auto-debited annually, many subscribers do not actively think about the policy until an accident occurs or they notice an unexpected bank debit. Families searching for this in 2026 are often checking whether their deceased or injured family member had active cover — or trying to file a claim.

Whether the need is proactive (check renewal before June) or reactive (start a claim process), the approach is the same: go to the bank where the account is held, collect the relevant documents, and work through the official process without shortcuts.

## Quick checklist

- Confirm the ₹20 annual premium (check the current premium with your bank — the scheme amount has been subject to revision) was successfully debited from your savings account around 1 June 2026.
- Verify the savings account linked to PMSBY is active, not dormant or zero-balance.
- Check that the nominee's name and relationship are correctly recorded with the bank.
- Ensure Aadhaar and PAN details in the bank records match your identity documents.
- If filing a claim for accidental death, gather the official FIR, post-mortem report, death certificate, and discharge summary where applicable.
- For disability claims, get the disability certificate from a government hospital or authorised medical authority.
- Do not rely on third-party agents for claim filing — approach the bank branch directly.

## Documents to keep ready

| Document | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Bank account passbook or statement | Confirms the annual premium debit and that the account remains active through the policy year. |
| Nominee details (original enrolment form) | The nominee name and relationship at the bank must be correct; an outdated record can delay or block a claim. |
| Premium debit proof | Bank SMS, passbook entry, or mini-statement for the current policy year confirming the ₹20 debit. |
| Identity proof (Aadhaar, PAN) | Required for beneficiary verification at the time of claim or policy record correction. |
| PAN and bank details | Useful for tax filing, refunds, benefit credits, and identity matching where applicable. |
| A short review note | Records what you checked, what is still pending, and which official source you used. |

## Practical example

A construction site supervisor enrolled in PMSBY through his Jan-Dhan account three years ago. He suffered a workplace fall in April 2026 that resulted in partial permanent disability. His family visited the bank and discovered that the policy had lapsed in June 2025 because the account balance was zero when the debit was attempted — he had stopped using that account and had no idea the debit was due. The claim was denied for the lapse period.

This is a preventable situation. A five-minute check each May — confirming the account balance is above ₹20 before the debit date, and reviewing the nominee detail — would have kept the cover active. If you hold PMSBY through a Jan-Dhan account you no longer use regularly, set a reminder and fund the account before 1 June each year.

## Reviewer guidance for PMSBY cases

When reviewing a PMSBY file, check: the policy year the cover was active for, the date of the accident relative to the policy period, the premium debit confirmation from the bank, and the nominee or claimant's identity documents. If the accident resulted in a claim, separately note whether the claim was submitted to the bank within the 30-day intimation window (or longer, as per current scheme guidelines) and which documents the bank has already received.

Where the review also touches AY 2026-27 ITR — for example, if compensation received under PMSBY needs to be reported or if the deceased's estate has ongoing tax obligations — note the income head, ITR form, tax regime, TDS credit, and e-verification status separately.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is PMSBY eligibility guaranteed by this guide?

No. Eligibility depends on the official portal, current scheme rules, state or ministry verification, and the applicant's documents.

### Should I use only social media information before applying?

No. Use social posts only to identify the issue, then verify the rule and application status on official government sources.

### Why keep tax records for a government scheme?

Many applications ask for income, bank, identity, or business records. A clean document trail reduces avoidable mismatch and follow-up questions.

## Final takeaway

PMSBY's accident cover is inexpensive and genuinely useful for workers who are not covered by employer insurance. Its one weakness is that it requires active maintenance — confirming the annual debit has gone through and the nominee record is current. Do not wait for a claim situation to discover the cover lapsed. Check once a year, every May, before the June debit window.
