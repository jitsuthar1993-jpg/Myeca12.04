# PMSBY Accident Insurance Checklist for Workers and Families

A PMSBY file should show the enrolled account, premium debit, nominee, coverage-period record, and documents required if a claim arises. Keep the debit proof, but do not treat it as a substitute for current enrolment or claim evidence.

For PMSBY, record the coverage period and the accident-related evidence that would be required if an insured event occurs. The file should distinguish enrolment and premium debit from accident intimation, medical or disability evidence, and claimant or nominee documents. Do not assume that the life-insurance claim path or records used for PMJJBY answer the accident-specific questions. If an accident occurs, create a dated chronology of intimation, treatment, certification, police or authority records where applicable, and communication with the bank or insurer. That chronology should identify the claimed event and resulting loss without treating a premium debit as proof of the accident, disability, or claim amount.

Connect accident-cover enrolment to the applicable coverage period, then keep any accident or disability claim in its own dated chronology. The claim file should identify the event, intimation, medical or disability evidence, authority record where applicable, claimant or nominee details, and bank or insurer response. A premium debit does not prove the accident, resulting loss, or claim amount, and life-insurance documents do not replace accident-specific evidence.

## Confirm accident-cover enrolment for the relevant period

A PMSBY review should first establish the enrolled account, applicable coverage period, premium debit, nominee information, and recognised status. Check those records while the account holder can correct them. A debit entry can support the payment history, but it does not prove every coverage condition, nominee detail, accident, disability, or claim amount. Read the current official route for enrolment, renewal, exit, and claim handling when a decision is required.

If the account changes, a debit fails, or nominee information appears wrong, raise the issue through the bank or applicable insurance channel and retain the response. Do not use life-insurance records or another account's history to infer accident cover. The person should know which account and period the file concerns before an insured event ever occurs.

## Separate enrolment evidence from accident-specific proof

The account holder owns the accuracy of enrolment details supplied. The bank and insurer control enrolment, premium transactions, recognised updates, and claim processing. Hospitals, doctors, police, and other authorities own the records they issue after an accident where applicable. A premium debit cannot establish the event or resulting loss, and a medical record cannot by itself establish coverage.

Preserve enrolment acknowledgement, coverage-period information, premium debit, nominee details, and update responses. If an accident occurs, open a separate claim file containing the event chronology, intimation, medical or disability evidence, authority records where applicable, claimant or nominee material, submission, and responses. Keep the original event description and later assessments together rather than replacing one with the other. Share only records relevant to the accident claim route.

## Build the claim chronology from event to response

Track the accident date, immediate treatment or authority record, first intimation, formal claim submission, acknowledgement, requests for information, medical or disability certification, insurer or bank response, and payment or grievance. Note who owns each pending action. This chronology should make the claimed event and resulting loss understandable without asking a reviewer to infer them from a premium transaction.

For enrolment issues, maintain a separate timeline by coverage period and account. If a claim is rejected, delayed, or corrected, preserve the stated reason and every response without implying an outcome beyond the official record. The completed file should let the insured person or nominee demonstrate what cover was recorded, what accident occurred, what evidence was issued, and how the claim progressed while keeping PMSBY distinct from life-cover or unrelated medical matters.

Before a claim submission, reconcile the event description across medical, police, disability, and claimant records where those documents are applicable and genuinely available. Note any difference instead of rewriting an issuer's record. Prepare a claim index showing the document, issuer, date, and fact it supports, while keeping unrelated treatment material out. If the insurer asks for clarification, answer the named inconsistency and preserve the request. This makes the accident file coherent without fabricating causation, severity, or claim value.

Keep that PMSBY Accident Insurance page and its check date with the application record, and route an error in the underlying source to the issuer or programme channel that owns the disputed fact.

## Verify the accident-cover period and nominee trail

For PMSBY, connect the account holder, enrolment or consent record, premium debit, coverage period, nominee details, and participating bank communication. Treat the accident-cover record separately from life-cover or pension enrolments that may use the same account. A premium debit can support payment but does not by itself prove every coverage, nominee, or claim fact.

Review account changes, failed or duplicate debits, nominee updates, and the coverage-period record through the bank or official channel. Keep the earlier enrolment and acknowledged correction so the history remains traceable. Family members should know where the policy and nominee information is retained without receiving unnecessary account credentials.

If a claim event occurs, build a dated file from the enrolment record, coverage evidence, nominee details, required event documents, submission, and follow-up. Claim assessment and payment remain with the participating bank and insurer. Escalate an unclear coverage period, inconsistent nominee, missing debit explanation, or unofficial request for credentials or payment.
