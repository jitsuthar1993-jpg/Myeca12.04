# PM Fasal Bima Yojana Claim Document Checklist for 2026

When a crop fails, filing a PM Fasal Bima Yojana claim should be straightforward — but it rarely is for farmers who have not kept their insurance and land records in order. The scheme covers yield losses, post-harvest losses, and localised calamities, but the claim process requires matching your policy acknowledgement, land details, loss intimation, and bank records precisely. A mismatch at any step can result in a delayed or rejected credit.

This guide is for farmers in 2026 who want to understand what documents are needed before they file a claim under PM Fasal Bima Yojana. It does not guarantee approval, claim amount, or processing time — those depend on the insurance company, state government, and crop-cutting experiment results.

## The claims process and where records go wrong

PMFBY operates through insurance companies empanelled by state governments. The premium is shared between the farmer, the state, and the central government. When a loss occurs, the farmer must intimate the insurer within 72 hours for localised calamities (like hailstorm or landslide). For widespread yield failure, the claim is triggered automatically through crop-cutting experiments — but the bank account registered at enrolment must be current and Aadhaar-linked for the credit to go through.

Two common problems: first, the name on the policy does not match the Aadhaar or bank account because the enrolment was done through a Common Service Centre without proper KYC. Second, the land records (khatauni or ROR) used at enrolment do not match current ownership, especially after inheritance or subdivision.

## Before the season starts — things to verify

- Confirm your enrolment status on the PMFBY portal using your application number or policy number.
- Check that the crop, area, and land parcel on the policy match your current ROR or Jamabandi.
- Ensure the bank account is Aadhaar-seeded and that the account number on file with the insurer is accurate.
- Note the loss intimation deadline for your state and season — it varies by state and crop type.

## A real-world scenario

A cotton farmer in Maharashtra discovers significant crop damage after unseasonal rain. He calls the Crop Insurance Company helpline within 48 hours to intimate the loss. Three months later, when neighbouring farmers receive their claim amounts, his credit does not come through. On checking, it turns out his bank account on the PMFBY portal still shows a joint account that was closed the previous year. The claim was processed, but the transfer failed — and reopening it required additional paperwork and a visit to the district agriculture office.

Keeping the bank account updated with the insurer each season takes five minutes. Undoing a failed credit transfer takes months.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is PM Fasal Bima Yojana eligibility guaranteed by this guide?

No. Eligibility depends on the official portal, current scheme rules, state or ministry verification, and the applicant's documents.

### Should I use only social media information before applying?

No. Use social posts only to identify the issue, then verify the rule and application status on official government sources.

### Why keep tax records for a government scheme?

Many applications ask for income, bank, identity, or business records. A clean document trail reduces avoidable mismatch and follow-up questions.

## Bottom line

A PMFBY claim is only as strong as the records behind it. Get your policy acknowledgement, land records, bank account details, and loss intimation dated and in one folder — before the season, not after the damage.
