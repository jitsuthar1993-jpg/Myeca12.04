# Foreign Remittance Form 15CA and 15CB Document Readiness

Getting a foreign remittance through the bank is ultimately a document exercise — but the compliance work happens well before you walk into the branch. Whether you are paying a foreign vendor, settling a royalty, or repatriating funds, the payer must first settle the questions of taxability, withholding, purpose code, and the correct Form 15CA part. Form 15CB may or may not be needed, depending on the facts.

Leaving all of this to the day of remittance is how avoidable delays happen.

## Start with remittance facts

Before anything else, write down the core details: remitter identity, remittee name and country, payment amount and currency, the commercial purpose, the underlying invoice or agreement, the proposed remittance date, and whether the payment is chargeable to tax in India under the Income Tax Act, 1961.

These facts are not just administrative housekeeping. They directly determine which part of Form 15CA applies, and whether a CA-certified Form 15CB is required at all. Purpose code selection also flows from these same facts, so getting clarity early prevents mismatches later.

## Prepare documents

Assemble the full document set before approaching your bank or CA:

- Invoice and underlying commercial agreement
- Remitter's PAN and bank details
- Remittee's details, including country of residence
- Tax residency certificate, where applicable
- Form 10F context, where applicable
- Lower-deduction certificate, if one has been obtained
- Taxability and DTAA working note
- CA assignment confirmation, where Form 15CB is involved

The bank will not wait while you chase documents. Having everything in one folder before the remittance review begins makes coordination significantly faster.

## Form 15CB coordination

Form 15CB is a CA certificate confirming that TDS has been correctly deducted and the remittance is otherwise in order. It is not a blanket requirement for every foreign payment. Some remittances are exempt, some fall below specified thresholds, and some are covered under specific carve-outs. Assuming it is always required wastes time; assuming it is never required is a compliance risk.

Where Form 15CB is needed, coordinate early with the signing CA. Provide the full document package, clarify the taxability position, and ensure the CA has the treaty details if a DTAA benefit is being claimed. After the certificate is issued, preserve the original along with the acknowledgement trail from the income tax portal.
