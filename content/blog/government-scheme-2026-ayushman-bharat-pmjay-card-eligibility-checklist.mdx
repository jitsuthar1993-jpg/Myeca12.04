# Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY Card Eligibility Checklist for Families

A PM-JAY check starts with beneficiary and household records rather than a promise of hospital cover. Confirm the family entry and accepted identity route on the official portal, then keep the card or reference details separate from hospital bills and treatment records.

## PMJAY eligibility: Start with the beneficiary and household question

The applicant's first decision is whether the immediate task is checking a beneficiary record, correcting household details, or preparing for treatment at an empanelled facility. Do not begin with a promise that a card guarantees a particular hospital expense. Confirm the household or beneficiary entry through the official route and identify the accepted identity path for that person. Compare names, relationships, and other displayed household details carefully before asking for a card or presenting the record during care.

If the person is not found or a family detail is wrong, capture the exact result and determine which authority or support channel controls that beneficiary information. A hospital bill, bank record, or general identity document may support a later query, but none automatically creates or repairs the programme's household entry. Avoid duplicate household attempts made only to bypass a mismatch; they can make the underlying correction harder to trace.

Before treatment, preserve the beneficiary or card reference and note the facility contacted and response received. During care, keep clinical and financial records in the hospital's normal sequence, including any authorisation or denial communication provided through the applicable channel. If a name or household mismatch blocks use, ask whether the issue belongs to the beneficiary record, identity validation, or hospital entry. Direct the correction accordingly instead of changing unrelated medical or bank documents.

## Follow status and treatment as two dated trails

Maintain one chronology for beneficiary verification and another for the hospital episode. The beneficiary trail should show the search or verification date, reference, correction request, response, and final displayed status. The treatment trail should show the facility, admission or consultation date, authorisation communication where applicable, services, bills, discharge material, and any later query. Linking the two by date is useful, but merging them obscures who owns each event.

If a query remains unresolved, state the beneficiary reference, household issue, facility, and specific stage at which the problem arose. Keep formal acknowledgements and responses rather than relying only on screenshots or verbal assurances. After resolution, confirm both the beneficiary details and the hospital record. This approach supports follow-up without representing a beneficiary check as an approval of treatment or a hospital bill as proof of programme status.

Where treatment cannot wait for a record correction, preserve the facility's explanation of how the episode was handled and what follow-up remains. Do not alter medical dates to match a later beneficiary update. Linking the correction and treatment timelines honestly allows a reviewer to see the urgency, the records available at the time, and the later administrative resolution without confusing clinical need with programme status.
