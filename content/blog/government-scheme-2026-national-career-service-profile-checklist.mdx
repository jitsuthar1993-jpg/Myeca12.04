# National Career Service Profile Checklist for Job Seekers

A National Career Service profile should make each education, experience, skill, and contact claim verifiable. Remove outdated claims, keep certificates behind the qualifications listed, and use a mobile and email account that the job seeker can access for employer contact.



## Decide which claims belong on the job-seeker profile

A National Career Service profile should represent the work the job seeker can presently support, not an aspirational biography. Begin with the target role or employment direction, then review education, experience, skills, location, availability, mobile number, and email. Keep a certificate, employment record, portfolio item, or clear explanation behind each material claim. Remove outdated contact details and claims that can no longer be demonstrated. A shorter accurate profile is more useful than a broad one that creates doubt during employer contact.

Decide how much personal information is necessary for matching and communication. Use a mobile number and email account the job seeker can access and monitor. Do not invent experience, alter dates, or claim a qualification that is only planned. If a credential or employer record contains an error, correct or clarify it with the issuer before using it as the basis for the profile.

## Keep profile facts, credentials, and employer actions separate

The job seeker owns the profile entries and application choices. Schools, training providers, and former employers own the records they issued. NCS controls the platform profile and its transaction history, while a prospective employer owns its vacancy, screening, interview, and hiring decision. A profile update cannot repair an incorrect certificate, and a platform match does not establish that an employer offered or filled a role.

Preserve the profile version used for important applications. When a qualification, experience date, skill, location, or contact detail changes, record the update and keep the supporting source. If an employer questions a claim, answer with the relevant evidence or correct the profile openly. Report suspicious or inaccurate vacancy information through the appropriate channel rather than sharing credentials or sensitive identity material with an unverified contact.

## Follow each opportunity from contact to outcome

Maintain a chronology for significant applications: vacancy or employer, profile version, application date, communication channel, screening request, interview, offer or rejection, and any reported issue. Note whether a missed contact resulted from an outdated profile field or from the employer's process. This distinction shows what the job seeker can correct and what belongs to the employer or platform.

After a profile change, monitor the accessible contact channels and confirm that future applications use the current version. Keep offers and employment records outside the general profile history so they can be verified independently. A sound follow-up file should demonstrate what the job seeker claimed, which issuer supports it, who contacted the applicant, and what happened next without treating registration, a match, or an interview as guaranteed employment.

Periodically review whether the target role still matches the evidence and availability shown. If the job seeker completes training, gains experience, moves location, or changes working preferences, update only the affected claims and record the source. If a qualification remains pending, describe it as pending rather than completed. This keeps employer conversations focused on current, supportable facts and prevents stale profile language from creating an avoidable verification problem.

## National Career Service Profile: source pages and next actions

Read [National Career Service official portal](https://www.ncs.gov.in/) for the current instruction affecting accurate job profile with education, experience, skills, and verified contact records. Keep that National Career Service Profile page and its check date with the application record, and route an error in the underlying source to the issuer or programme channel that owns the disputed fact.
