# PMJJBY Insurance Renewal and Nominee Checklist for 2026

A PMJJBY review should confirm enrolment, premium debit, account holder, nominee, and the records needed for a later claim. A debit entry may show payment but not prove that nominee or claim details are current, so keep the enrolment and bank evidence together.

For PMJJBY, separate evidence of life-insurance enrolment from evidence needed after the insured person's death. Review the annual premium debit, enrolment period, nominee entry, and bank communication while the account holder can still correct them. A later claim file will need the applicable death and claimant records; an old debit entry alone cannot establish a complete claim. Read the current renewal and exit instructions before the annual debit window, especially where the account has changed or lacked funds. Record the bank branch or channel used for any nominee correction, and keep the acknowledgement with the enrolment record so a claimant is not left to reconstruct the account holder's instructions from memory.

Keep life-cover enrolment and a later death-claim file separate. While the account holder can act, verify the enrolment period, premium debit, nominee entry, bank channel, and acknowledged changes. If a claim event occurs, build a dated file from the coverage record, death and claimant documents, submission, and insurer or bank follow-up. A debit can support payment history, but it does not establish every coverage or claim fact.

## Review life-cover enrolment while the account holder can act

The immediate decision for PMJJBY is whether the account holder's enrolment, applicable coverage period, bank account, premium debit, nominee entry, and contact details are current and understood. Review the recognised enrolment record and the bank statement together, especially before the relevant annual debit or renewal stage. A successful debit can support payment history, but it does not by itself prove that every coverage, nominee, or claim detail is correct.

If the account has changed, lacked funds, or shows an unexpected debit result, ask the bank or official channel for the applicable status and current instruction. Resolve nominee or account-detail concerns while the holder can provide and confirm information. Do not assume that an old debit, an unrelated insurance policy, or a general benefit description guarantees current cover or a future claim outcome.

## Separate enrolment records from a later death claim

The account holder owns the accuracy of enrolment and nominee information supplied. The bank and applicable insurance channel control enrolment, premium transaction, recognised updates, and claim handling. Civil and medical record issuers own the documents they issue after a death. A nominee correction belongs in the enrolment trail; a death claim requires its own claimant, event, submission, and response record. Neither should overwrite the other.

Preserve the enrolment acknowledgement, applicable-period information, premium debit, nominee details, and every update response. If an entry is wrong, record the earlier value and correction acknowledgement. If a death occurs, open a separate claim file containing the coverage record relied on, death and claimant documents required by the official route, submission reference, and insurer or bank communication. Avoid treating the debit entry as proof of every claim fact.

## Follow each coverage period and any claim chronology

Maintain a dated enrolment trail showing initial enrolment, each relevant premium debit or failure, account or nominee changes, and confirmed status. For an unexpected debit or missing debit, identify the period, account condition, channel contacted, and response. Preserve the result even if it shows that no corrective action or claim is available; the record should reflect the actual position.

If a claim event occurs, track the death date, document issuance, claimant submission, acknowledgement, requests for information, response, and payment or grievance separately. At each stage, contact the owner controlling the pending action. The final file should enable the holder or nominee to trace what was enrolled, what changed, and how a claim was handled, without using a payment transaction as a substitute for coverage confirmation or event-specific evidence.

The nominee or family should prepare a short handover note identifying the enrolled account, applicable period, latest nominee record, and location of the key acknowledgements. Keep it with the private file rather than treating it as a claim submission. This practical record reduces the chance that a later claimant relies only on a passbook entry or misses a correction the account holder completed before the event.
